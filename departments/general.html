Security for Everyone · Protecting Our Organization

Essential security practices for all team members across the organization. Learn how to recognize social engineering, protect company data, work safely remotely, and report incidents quickly.

General Organization Policies

1. Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)

Guidelines for corporate systems, internet, and personal device (BYOD) usage.

  • Company hardware & accounts are strictly for authorized business operations.
  • No unauthorized software, torrenting, or bypass proxies permitted.
  • Social media policy: Do not post confidential work details or internal photos.
  • Maintain email hygiene; do not use work email for personal registrations.

2. Data Classification & Handling Policy

Proper categorizing and safeguarding organizational information.

  • Public: Marketing content approved for external distribution.
  • Internal: Intranet documents, policies, internal Slack chats.
  • Confidential: Financial reports, customer databases, contracts.
  • Restricted: PII, API tokens, passwords, security keys.

3. Physical Security Policy

Securing physical offices, workstations, and visitor access.

  • Wear employee ID badges visibly at all times in office premises.
  • Clean Desk Policy: Lock physical documents and laptops when away from desk.
  • Prevent tailgating; do not allow non-badged visitors to follow you through doors.
  • Use secure cross-cut shredder bins for paper documents containing sensitive data.

4. Remote & Hybrid Work Policy

Maintaining security while working from home or public places.

  • Always connect through company VPN when using public or home Wi-Fi networks.
  • Use privacy screen filters in public cafes, airports, or trains.
  • Never leave work devices unattended in vehicles or public spaces.
  • Ensure home Wi-Fi routers use WPA3/WPA2 encryption with strong passwords.

5. Incident Reporting Policy

Mandatory steps when security incidents or suspicious events occur.

  • Report lost/stolen laptops or mobile phones immediately to IT Security.
  • Report suspicious emails via the PhishAlarm button or security email.
  • No-blame culture: Always report accidental clicks or mistakes immediately.
  • 24/7 Security Hotline: +1-800-555-SECURE / security@company.com.

Core Security Theory

Social Engineering Fundamentals

How attackers manipulate human trust and psychology.

  • Common tactics: Urgency ("Act within 10 mins!"), Authority ("CEO request"), Fear, and Curiosity.
  • Pretexting: Attackers creating elaborate fake scenarios to gain trust.
  • Always verify identity through an independent, verified out-of-band communication channel.

Phishing, Smishing & Vishing

Recognizing multi-channel scam attempts.

  • Phishing (Email): Lookalike domains (e.g. micros0ft.com), grammatical errors, fake urgency.
  • Smishing (SMS): Fake delivery tracking links or security alert texts.
  • Vishing (Voice / Phone): Scammers impersonating IT support or bank representatives.

MFA & Push Bombing Defense

Securing multi-factor authentication systems.

  • MFA requires two or more factors: Something you know, have, or are.
  • Push Bombing Attacks: Attackers spamming 50+ login approvals to force accidental 'Approve' click.
  • Never approve unrequested MFA push prompts. Always report them to Security.

Password Security & Password Managers

Best practices for credential hygiene.

  • Never reuse passwords across personal and work applications.
  • Use the approved company Password Manager (1Password / Bitwarden) to generate 16+ character passphrases.
  • Never write down passwords on post-it notes or unencrypted text files on desktop.

Data Privacy & PII Protection

Handling Personally Identifiable Information (PII).

  • PII includes SSNs, full names, home addresses, phone numbers, and financial details.
  • Compliance mandates: GDPR, CCPA require data minimization and strict access controls.
  • Never send unencrypted PII via external email or public chat channels.

Real-World General Scenarios

Scenario 1: Urgent CEO Email Request for Gift Cards High Urgency

You receive an urgent email signed by the company CEO stating: "I'm in a conference and need 10 Apple gift cards for clients immediately. Send codes via email."

Red Flags: Urgency, request for gift cards, external sender domain typo.

Action: Do NOT purchase cards or reply. Call the CEO on a known official phone number or verify with HR. Report the email to security.

Scenario 2: MFA Push Notification Bombing Critical Severity

Your phone receives 20 consecutive authenticator push notifications at 2:00 AM while you are asleep or relaxing.

Threat: An attacker has obtained your primary password and is attempting to fatigue you into tapping 'Approve'.

Action: Reject all prompts. Log in immediately and change your primary password. Report the incident to IT Security.

Scenario 3: Tailgating at Office Entrance Medium Severity

A person wearing delivery uniform holding large boxes follows closely behind you as you scan your badge at the secure office door.

Threat: Physical security breach via tailgating.

Action: Politely ask them to wait or swipe their own badge, or direct them to reception. Do not hold the door open for non-badged individuals.

Scenario 4: USB Drive Found in Parking Lot High Severity

You find a USB flash drive labeled "Executive Salaries 2026" sitting on the sidewalk near the building entrance.

Threat: USB Drop Attack containing automated malware/keylogger execution triggers.

Action: Do NOT plug the drive into any work or personal computer. Turn the drive directly over to the Security team.

Scenario 5: Deepfake Voice Call from Executive Critical Severity

You receive a phone call where the CFO's voice orders an emergency wire transfer to a new supplier bank account.

Threat: AI Deepfake Voice Cloning scam.

Action: Hang up and initiate verbal verification using official corporate phone channels and dual-authorization protocols before initiating financial transfers.
