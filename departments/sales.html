Secure Sales · Protecting Customer Trust

Security guidelines for sales representatives, account executives, and business development reps. Protect prospect PII, pipeline data, CRM accounts, and guard against Business Email Compromise.

Sales Security Policies

1. Customer Data Protection Policy

Handling client PII, lead lists, and contact records.

  • Store prospect & customer data exclusively in the corporate CRM (Salesforce/HubSpot).
  • No exporting lead lists to local unencrypted CSV/Excel spreadsheets on laptops.
  • Enforce Principle of Least Privilege: Access restricted to assigned accounts/territories.
  • Obtain proper opt-in consent before adding prospects to sales communication cadences.

2. Secure Sales Communication Policy

Approved communication channels and file sharing practices.

  • Use corporate email or approved messaging tools (Slack Connect, Teams) for deal chats.
  • Never share internal price lists or discount matrices via unencrypted public links.
  • Obtain explicit client consent before recording sales calls (Gong/Chorus compliance).
  • Encrypt emails containing sensitive contract annexes or security documentation.

3. Proposal & Contract Security Policy

Protecting commercial terms, SLAs, and signature workflows.

  • Use standard, approved legal templates for Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs) and MSAs.
  • Execute all contracts via enterprise e-signature platforms (DocuSign / Adobe Sign).
  • Never send unsigned contract drafts containing custom security concessions without legal approval.
  • Store executed contracts in the secure central repository.

4. BYOD & Mobile CRM Policy

Securing mobile devices used for field sales operations.

  • Mobile CRM access requires Mobile Device Management (MDM) enrollment and biometric/passcode lock.
  • Do not download or store customer contact databases locally on personal phones.
  • Immediate reporting required if a sales mobile device or tablet is lost or stolen.

5. Sales Travel Security Policy

Protecting company assets and deal info while traveling.

  • Always enable company VPN when connecting to hotel, airport, or conference Wi-Fi.
  • Use privacy screens on laptops during flights or public transport to block visual eavesdropping.
  • Never leave laptops or sales documents unattended in hotel rooms or rental cars.

Sales Security Theory

Customer Trust & Security as a Differentiator

Why security assurance wins enterprise deals.

  • Enterprise prospects evaluate security credentials (SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, GDPR compliance).
  • Security transparency builds trust and accelerates sales cycles.
  • A security breach destroys customer trust and leads to contract cancellations.

Responding to Security Questionnaires

Best practices for completing prospect SIG/CAIQ questionnaires.

  • Never guess or make up answers regarding security controls or encryption protocols.
  • Direct complex technical inquiries to the Security Compliance team.
  • Utilize the official Trust Center portal for sharing SOC 2 reports under NDA.

Social Engineering Directed at Sales

Why sales reps are prime targets for impersonation attacks.

  • Sales Reps are public-facing and naturally eager to engage with new inbound contacts.
  • Attacker tactic: Impersonating high-value prospects to send malicious RFP attachments (.exe / macro-enabled docs).
  • Urgency traps: "Sign this contract today, click this link to verify bank info."

Business Email Compromise (BEC) in Sales

How BEC fraud disrupts sales transactions.

  • Attackers compromise vendor or buyer emails to insert fake banking/wire instructions.
  • Invoice Fraud: Sending altered invoices before quarterly deal closing deadlines.
  • Always confirm bank detail changes verbally with the buyer's finance team using known phone numbers.

CRM Access Controls & Data Hygiene

Preventing pipeline data exfiltration.

  • Export restrictions prevent disgruntled or departing reps from stealing lead lists.
  • Enforce mandatory MFA for all CRM user accounts.
  • Revoke CRM access immediately upon employee departure.

Real-World Sales Scenarios

Scenario 1: Inbound RFP with Suspicious Attachment High Severity

An inbound lead sends an email: "We want to award a $500k contract. Download our custom requirement specification executable here: RFP_Specs.exe".

Threat: Malware delivery via executable masquerading as a sales RFP.

Action: Do NOT open or download `.exe` or executable files. Report the email to IT Security for analysis.

Scenario 2: Wire Transfer Routing Change Request Critical Severity

Before closing a major deal, an email from the client's procurement department requests sending payment to a updated offshore bank account.

Threat: Business Email Compromise (BEC) / Payment Hijacking.

Action: Do NOT update bank details based on email alone. Verbally confirm bank details via phone with the client's verified controller.

Scenario 3: Client Demands Internal Architecture Diagrams Medium Severity

A prospect insists on receiving detailed internal cloud network topology diagrams before signing a contract.

Action: Check if an executed NDA is in place. Engage the Security team to share sanitized architecture overviews rather than raw internal infrastructure diagrams.

Scenario 4: Fake Salesforce Login Phishing Page High Severity

You receive an email claiming "Your Salesforce password expires in 1 hour". The link opens a page looking identical to Salesforce login.

Threat: Credential harvesting targeting CRM accounts.

Action: Check the browser URL bar (e.g. `login-salesforce-verify.com` vs official domain). Close the tab and report the email to Security.

Scenario 5: Laptop Stolen at Conference Hotel Critical Severity

While attending a sales conference, your laptop containing cached prospect leads is stolen from the hotel lobby.

Action: Notify IT Security immediately so they can issue a remote wipe command and revoke active CRM tokens.
