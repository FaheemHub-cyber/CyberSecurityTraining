You don’t need to be a coder to stay safe

Security is everyone’s job. Designers, product managers, marketers, and executives all handle sensitive data, third‑party tools, and company credentials. This guide covers the real‑world risks you face every day — and what to do about them.

Social Engineering

Attackers manipulate people, not code. They use urgency, fear, authority, or curiosity to make you click, share, or approve something you shouldn’t.

#1 threat vector

Figma & Design Files

Design files often contain brand assets, user flows, and even API mock data. Never share Figma links publicly. Use view‑only links with expiry dates.

protect your work

Phishing Emails

Look for urgency, bad grammar, mismatched sender addresses, and unexpected attachments. Hover over links before clicking. When in doubt, verify via a separate channel.

90% of breaches start here

MFA Push Bombing

Attackers spam you with MFA requests until you accidentally approve one. Never approve a push you didn’t trigger. Use number‑matching or hardware keys.

Uber 2022 breach

Third‑Party Apps (Slack, Notion, Miro…)

Every connected app is a potential backdoor. Audit which apps have access to your data. Remove unused apps. Use SSO where possible and enforce MFA.

supply chain risk

Mac & Device Security

Keep your OS and apps updated. Enable FileVault (disk encryption). Use a strong passcode and never leave your device unlocked in public.

physical security matters

Vishing (Voice Phishing)

Attackers call pretending to be IT, vendors, or executives. Hang up and call back using a known, verified number. Never give out MFA codes.

MGM Resorts $100M loss

USB Baiting

Never plug in a USB drive you found. It could contain malware that compromises your machine and spreads across the network.

Stuxnet classic

Deepfakes & AI Voice Cloning

Attackers now use synthetic video and voice to impersonate executives. Treat any unusual request from a “video call” with skepticism — verify out‑of‑band.

$25M deepfake heist

Tailgating (Physical)

Don’t hold the door open for strangers — even if they look like employees. Badge‑in securely and report anyone without a badge.

physical access = digital access
Real scenario
A designer shares a Figma link on a public Slack channel. An attacker finds it, extracts the embedded API key from a mock, and compromises the dev environment. Fix: Use view‑only links with expiry and never put real credentials in design files.
